## Part A. Notices you can always close

### Where it stands (read from the code; not yet reproduced on the phone)

- A landing is two kinds of happening, `space.colony` and `space.arrival`, both of weight 6
  (`sim/space/space.ts`, `sim/space/voyages.ts`). Six is exactly the line for "great" (`GREAT`, `ui/events.ts`),
  so every colony founded and every ship come in opens an event window in the middle of the screen, and the
  clock waits behind it unless that was turned off.
- The window's only way out is **Let it be**, one button among four at its foot, under a why-tree whose first
  cause opens itself and lengthens the window. There is no ✕ in its title, a tap outside does nothing, and
  Escape does nothing. On a phone the window scrolls, so that button may sit below the fold (A0 measures it).
- In a spacefaring age they queue ("3 more waiting"), each wanting its own tap.
- A second notice, the card "🚀 Year N: a landing on …" (`ui/colonies.ts`), has no close at all. It stays 16
  seconds and comes back each time a world with settled worlds is opened.
- The one setting is all or nothing and lives in the window's own foot: great happenings told in windows, as
  cards, or not at all.

## Part B. Life from first principles

### Where it stands (measured 2026-11-12)

Three hundred open worlds, `alien 0` to `alien 299`, made at the full grid and not run
(scratchpad `life-tally.mts`; B0 moves it to `tools/`).

| What | As the rules stand |
|---|---|
| Worlds with life | 224 of 300. Every one of them has a people |
| What life is dissolved in | water 203, ammonia 11, oil 5, methane 5 |
| Body types a thinking people can have | **8, a fixed list.** Shaggy climbers 65, burrowers 31, upright apes 30, shore-walkers 27, crawlers 22, striders 22, swimmers 14, trunked giants 13 |
| How a people is drawn | 5 stances. 42% of peoples share the "low" one, 29% the "stooped" one |
| Peoples of other stars and natives | A plain draw from the same 8, not weighed by their world (`gen/civilizations.ts`, `sim/space/natives.ts`) |
| Roles a beast can fill | **8, the same on every world**: grazer, browser, great beast, seed-eater, swimmer, small hunter, scavenger, hunter. A world fills 6.6 of them |
| Plants | One role, seed grass |
| Beasts | 5,128 living lineages, 22.9 a world |
| Silhouettes (limbs and how it moves) | **15 across all 5,128.** The commonest five are 61% of every beast. A world shows 9.5 of the 15, so any two worlds share most of their shapes |
| Symmetry | two-sided 95%, radial 5% (swimmers only) |
| What a beast can grow | 11 things, all Earth's: horns, wide horns, antlers, tusks, a trunk, a hump, a mane, a crest, spines, claws, a beak |
| Small lives under the glass | 4 kinds, the same everywhere: eater, light-eater, hunter, colony |

How each layer is decided today:

1. **Whether life starts** (`gen/elsewhere.ts`, `rules/kinds.ts`): thresholds on warmth and the star's age, then a
   draw against fixed odds (0.85 in seas; by kind of world 0.6, 0.85, 0.7, 0.35). Nothing asks what energy there
   was, what it was made from, or where it began.
2. **What it is made of** (`rules/biochem.ts`): tables keyed by the world's kind. Five kinds give four liquids and
   five cell skins; bones, blood and breath come from short chains of `if kind is`. That is the "lists by kind"
   F3 removed from technology.
3. **Beasts** (`rules/creatures.ts`, `gen/biosphere.ts`): the eight roles are a list. A body is chosen for its
   role by nudged odds (a heavier pull makes more legs likelier), not by whether it can bear its weight or lift
   itself.
4. **The tree of life** (`gen/evolution.ts`) is the part worth keeping. Every lineage is bred by mutation and
   selection under fifteen pressures (water, air, cold, pull, the hunted, rivals…), and every change is recorded
   with what favoured it. Its alphabet is small and Earth's: legs, fins or wings; seven coverings; three gaits;
   every world starts from the same first worm and first jelly.
5. **The people** (`rules/bodies.ts`, `riseOf`): picked from the eight types by fit and chance. They are not taken
   from the tree. Only their forebears' growths, eyes, tail and neck are carried into their look.

So the owner's reading is right. The process is real; the parts list is short, Earth's, and written as lists.

### The rule

**A part says what it does. The world's physics says what can do it and what it costs. Selection picks. Nobody
writes a creature.**

1. **What a living thing must get done**: take in energy, hold its shape, move or stay, sense, feed, keep its
   warmth, breed. For a people as well: handle things, and talk.
2. **What a world offers**, read from its numbers and never from its kind's name: its pull; its air's weight and
   what is in it; its liquid and how thick that is; its light, how much and what colour; heat and chemistry from
   below; its seasons, its day, whether one face is locked to its star; how much is sea; which ingredients are
   scarce.
3. **How the two meet**: for each job, the means this world allows, each with a cost. A flier exists where lift
   beats weight. A giant exists where its frame can bear it. An eye exists where there is light worth seeing.

### The primitives

**1. How life starts.** Five needs, each a number: a liquid that lasts; a flow of energy; ingredients; a place
that gathers them; time. The places: seafloor vents; tide pools and shores that wet and dry; brine veins in
ice; hot springs and clays; sunlit films; hazes that rain out. Each has a rate from the world's numbers. The
first to succeed fixes where life began, what it first ate, and when. A world where it never did says what
was missing.

**2. What it runs on.** About a dozen trades of energy, each open only where the world holds both halves:

- light that splits the liquid (it frees an oxidant, which later allows fire and strong, fast bodies);
- light on sulfur, iron or hydrogen (frees none);
- dim red light (many catches for one fixing: slow);
- rock chemistry: hydrogen with carbon dioxide, sulfur, iron, ammonia; on methane worlds acetylene with hydrogen;
- breathing oxygen, nitrate, sulfate, or hydrogen;
- eating others, with or without something to breathe.

Each has a yield, and the yield sets **power for each kilogram**. Breathing oxygen gives about fifteen times
what fermenting does. Power decides how large an active body can be, whether warm blood pays, whether hunters
exist, how many links the chain of eating has (about a tenth passes up each link), and whether a brain can be
afforded. The date a world's air changes is worked out from when its light-eaters outran what soaks the
oxidant up.

**3. The body's parts.**

| Part | Choices |
|---|---|
| Symmetry | none; radial in 3, 4, 5, 6 or 8; two-sided; coiled; a colony of units |
| Segments | 1 to 12 repeated units, gathered into as many as three regions |
| Frame | none (held by its own pressure); an outer jointed shell; inner bones; a mesh of needles; a gas-filled frame. Of what its seas hold (the five bone stuffs there are) |
| Limbs | 0 to 12 pairs or rings, each with a **job**: strut, paddle, wing (skin, feather or plate), grasper (hand, pincer, tentacle, trunk, lip, tail), feeler, weapon, digger, anchor, sail. A limb can change its job down the generations |
| Moving | walk, run, hop, crawl, slither, burrow, climb, swim by wave, paddle, jet, drift, float on a gas bag, glide, fly, roll, rooted. Each allowed or shut by the world's physics |
| Feeding | light-eating (rooted or moving), filtering, grazing, browsing, digging, chasing, ambush, trap or lure, scavenging, rot-eating, living on another, carrying light-eaters in its skin |
| Senses | sight (its band set by the star and by what the air or liquid lets through; pit, cup, lens or many-faced eyes), hearing, echo, smell, touch, electric (only in a liquid that carries current), magnetic (only on a world with a field), heat |
| Covering | bare, slime, scales, plates, shell, filaments, feathers, bark, wax, mirror-bright, glowing. Its colour from the pigments its chemistry has and the light it lives in |
| Warmth | cold-blooded, warm-blooded, warm by sheer size; with coats and radiators |
| Size | from what its frame bears under the pull, its power, its warmth |
| Breeding | spawn, eggs, live young, budding, spores; a young form unlike the grown; one, two or many sexes; castes |
| Pace of life | many and quick, or few and slow (as now) |
| Show and defence | spikes, clubs, frills, sails, crests, lures, venom, ink, armour, warning colours, looking like something else; grown anywhere on the body |
| Company | alone, pairs, herds and shoals, packs, a hive |

**The checks that prune them**, each a pure function with real figures, each tested against Earth's own (an
elephant, a hummingbird, a squid and a beetle must come out possible on Earth; a six-tonne flier must not):

- what a frame bears against the pull (the largest land body for each frame);
- flight: lift against weight from the wing's area, the air's weight and the pull; floating: a lifting gas
  against the air's own weight;
- breathing: by tubes, skin, gills or lungs, each with a ceiling set by how much oxidant the air holds;
- swimming and jetting against the liquid's thickness;
- warmth: surface against bulk (the smallest warm body in the cold, the radiators the heat asks);
- senses: the light there is in each band at the surface and at depth; how far sound carries; whether the
  liquid carries a current; whether there is a field;
- rooted growths: how high the liquid can be lifted against the pull, and against the wind;
- breeding: eggs or live young by the medium and the cold.

**4. Founding stocks, a world's own signature.** Each world's first bodies are three to six stocks drawn from the
grammar under its own conditions. Everything after descends from them. Deep traits (symmetry, frame, how many
limbs and where) change about a hundred times more rarely than shallow ones (size, covering, proportions,
growths, colour). So one world's land beasts are all six-limbed and shelled, another's are three-legged with a
sail down the back, and each world's people look like kin of its beasts. This is why Earth's beasts look
related, and it is the main cure for seeing the same aliens: variety between worlds comes from which stocks a
world happened to start with, and likeness within a world comes from descent.

**5. Roles from the world.** A role is a place to live, a food and a size. The places a world may offer: the sunlit
sea, the deep, the sea floor, vents, shores, rivers, each kind of land, underground, the canopy where growths
stand tall, the air itself where it is thick, ice, the twilight belt of a locked world. How many roles fill
follows how much energy its light-eaters and rock-eaters fix. A rich world fills forty and more, five links
deep. A dim cold one fills a dozen and has no great hunters. Light-eaters become lineages too: mats, grasses,
trees, floaters, growths at vents. "Can be sown", "can be herded" and "can be ridden" stay, as tests any lineage
may pass, as mounts already are.

**6. Who rises to thought.** No list. A lineage qualifies by something to handle with that is free while it
stands; a sense and a signal to talk by (sound, colour and light, touch, scent, current); living together;
power for a brain; a long childhood; a size in range. The best rise, with chance, as now. Their `BodyPlan` is
read from their body. What follows is read by function, not by type: how finely they handle, whether they can
tend a heat, what arms and armour fit, what they wear, which parts a wound can take. Peoples of other stars and
natives are read the same way from their own worlds. A world where none qualifies has no people and says why.

**7. Seen and explained.** One builder draws beasts and peoples from the grammar (now two, with five stances for
peoples), with a few low-poly shapes added to the boxes (taper, dome, disc, tube chain, membrane, bag), gaits
worked out from where the limbs are, and the same cap of parts for each body. Every species has a page: its
body labelled by job, each trait with its why and its figure ("flies: air 3.1 bar, pull 0.6 g, lifts 40 kg"),
what it eats and what eats it, its line back to its stock. Every world has a Life page: where life began and
on what, when its air changed, its stocks, the roles filled and the roles empty with the reason. Two worlds can
be set side by side, as What can be done here already does.

### Measures and the gate

- **The tally's targets** (over the same 300 worlds; B0 confirms the exact lines):
  silhouettes from 15 to at least 150; the commonest five from 61% to under 25%; two worlds sharing under a
  third of their shapes; after B4, at least 60 different peoples' bodies with none above 10% (now 8, the
  commonest 29%). If physics converges harder than a target, the target is wrong: no variety is faked.
- **A count is not a sight.** The gate also draws each world's beasts and people at phone size and measures that
  the outlines differ on screen, and lays twelve worlds side by side as a sheet for the owner's eye.
- **The checks hold on every world**: no flier where lift is less than weight, no electric sense in a liquid
  that carries no current, no warm giant on a weak trade of energy.
- **Earth is pinned**: `first light`'s beasts, people and history identical at every step; the eight oracle
  fixtures unchanged through B0 to B2.
- **Probed on real worlds**, since a green test is no evidence a change fired: before and after on named seeds.
- **Before the bump lands**: the fixed-year gates (35, 51, 55, 55b, 56, 60, 66, 76, 79) and a fresh seed list.

### What stays fixed

Earth: its apes, its horses and sheep, its history, bit for bit. Figures come from real biology and physics,
and a page says plainly where one is an estimate (life in methane is one). No lists by kind of world. No random
draws added to history. Nothing stored: all of it is read from the world, so no save grows. The phone's budget:
the same cap of parts for a body, the same batches, a world opening no slower, the tree and the bodies worked
out off the main thread only when first looked at, and kept.

### Risks

- **Peoples who cannot play the game.** A body that cannot hold a tool would stall its world. Rising to thought
  asks for a grasper, so every people can reach tools by some means; F1 already straps arms along a limb.
- **Everything that reads a people's type.** About twenty places look a body up by its type's name, and wounds,
  armour and dress name arms, hands and legs. B4 gives them one reading by function first, proves Earth
  unchanged, and only then retires the types.
- **Old saves.** A saved alien world made under the old life must load as it was. As with kinds of world, an old
  save's seed text is marked to keep the old reading, and the old tables stay in the code, frozen.
- **A reading that disagrees with history** (B1, B2). The new body must keep every fact the simulation uses of a
  lineage: its role, weight, herd, fleece, where it lives. Named beasts' words are checked at spec time.
- **Cost on the phone.** Measured at each step with the pace gates, not assumed.

## Part C. Sizes

### Where it stands

- A world's grid is fixed at frequency 64: 40,962 cells (`PLANET_FREQUENCY`, `gen/homeworld.ts`). Its lands are on
  a grid of frequency 20: 4,002 over the whole globe. The seed list's worlds have 6 to 1,584 lands, 485 in the
  middle.
- The generator and the world's store already take a size, and lands already follow it
  (`sim/planet/store.ts`: lands' frequency is the grid's over 3.2, at most 20). Tests run small worlds this
  way. Only the app never asks for one.
- The stars: a cluster 50 light-years in radius at the Sun's neighbourhood's density (about two thousand stars,
  to be counted in C0) and at most 12 other peoples (`gen/galaxy.ts`, `gen/civilizations.ts`). Both fixed.
- The Quality setting changes only how richly things are drawn. Nothing lets a player choose how much is
  simulated.

### Plan

**Size is part of the world's making**, like its star and its pull: it is written in the seed's text, so the
same text is the same world on every device, links and saves carry it, and a world with no size written is
today's. Nothing that exists moves.

| Dial | Choices (lines to be set by C0's measures) | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| World | Small (grid 32: 10,242 cells, about a quarter of the lands), Medium (grid 48: 23,042 cells, about half), Standard (today), Large (grid 96, lands' cap raised; strong machines) | How many lands, realms, wars and towns there are to work out each year. The planet's true size and pull stay their own dial |
| Stars | Small (about 25 light-years, up to 4 other peoples), Standard (today), Large (about 80 light-years, up to 24) | How much there is to work out and draw in the star age |

| C4 Large worlds | Lands' cap raised for Large, with its own bounds for memory and saves; offered with a plain warning where the device is weak | Bigger worlds on a strong machine | Only Large worlds |

**Guards.**

- The device never picks a size silently: the same seed must be the same world on a phone and a PC. The title
  may recommend; the text says what was chosen.
- Standard stays the default everywhere, and the iPhone 15 Plus stays the floor for it.
- A small world must still be a true one: C0 compares the shape of history across sizes (people, ages reached,
  realms for each land, wars for each realm) before any size is offered.
- The seed list stays at Standard. A short list of Small worlds may follow, since it is cheap to run.

**A second use.** Small worlds make the checks cheaper: a history check that takes half an hour of a core on a
Standard world should take a few minutes on a Small one. That lowers what later work asks of this computer.
